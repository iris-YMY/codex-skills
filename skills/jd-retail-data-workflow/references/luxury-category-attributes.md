# Luxury category attribute framework

Select the category scope from the current brief. Do not assume that handbags, ready-to-wear, and footwear are always included, and do not exclude small leather goods merely because a previous project did so. When continuing an established project, reuse its confirmed scope unless the user changes it. Keep all selected categories separate in collection, charts, and conclusions.

| Category | Product dimensions to request |
|---|---|
| Handbags | bag type/silhouette, carrying mode, size, material, color, pattern/logo treatment, closure, gender, price band |
| Ready-to-wear | product type, fit/silhouette, fabric/material, color, pattern, length, season, gender, size, price band |
| Footwear | shoe type, toe shape, heel type/height, upper material, color, closure, season, gender, size, price band |
| Small leather goods | product type, size, material, color, pattern/logo treatment, closure, gender, price band |

Four evidence levels must not be confused:

- **Vocabulary:** official category attribute names and allowed values.
- **Own-SKU assignment:** attributes attached to Burberry or another own product.
- **Industry performance:** sales, share, growth, rank, or conversion by attribute from Shangzhi.
- **Keyword theme:** consumer wording inferred from search terms.

Only industry performance can directly support a claim such as “black handbags gained share.” Product counts, titles, competitor images, and keyword themes can provide context but not substitute for the performance measure.
